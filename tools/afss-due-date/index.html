The one after

    The calendar file has two all-day events, the due date (with reminders 30 and 7 days before) and the day the assessment window opens. It is made on your device.

    Critical measures run on their own cycle. If the fire safety schedule marks any measure as a critical fire safety measure, it also needs a supplementary fire safety statement at the interval the schedule sets, with the assessment dated within the 1 month before each supplementary statement is issued. This helper does not work those dates out.

    How it works

    • Due date. The owner must give the council an annual fire safety statement each year (s 89 of the Regulation). It is due 12 months after the last annual statement was given or, if a fire safety certificate was issued within the previous 12 months, 12 months after that certificate, whichever is later (s 89(2), as summarised by Fire Protection Association Australia). For the last statement or certificate date, the helper adds 12 calendar months. A due date the council holds is used as entered.
    • Assessment window. The notes to the approved fire safety statement form say the measures must be assessed, and the exits inspected, within the 3 months before the date the annual statement is issued (s 88). The helper counts the window from the day after the date 3 months before issue, so a date it shows is safely inside.
    • After issue. A copy goes to the council, and a copy with the current fire safety schedule goes to Fire and Rescue NSW as soon as practicable. A copy with the schedule is displayed in the building (s 89).
    • Month ends. When the month it lands in is shorter, the helper uses the last day of that month: 12 months after 29 February 2024 is 28 February 2025, and 3 months before 31 May is the end of February. The Regulation doesn't spell this out, so check with the council.
    • Late statements. Councils can issue a penalty notice for each week a statement is overdue (Schedule 1 of the Regulation). If you're late or about to be, contact the council.

    What this helper can't tell you

    • The council's own date. Councils word the 12-month rule differently: 12 months from when the last statement was lodged, from when it was issued, or a fixed anniversary. The date the council holds for the building is the one that counts, so confirm it.
    • Whether lodging early moves the next date. Where a council counts from the date it received the last statement, giving it one early can bring the next due date forward.
    • Extensions. Some councils say they can't extend the due date; others may give a short extension in some cases. Ask yours.
    • Supplementary statements for critical fire safety measures, which follow the intervals on the fire safety schedule.
