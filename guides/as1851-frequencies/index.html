AS 1851 routine service frequencies at a glance

Last reviewed 25 October 2026 · AS 1851-2012 incorporating Amendment 1 (2016)

AS 1851-2012 Routine service of fire protection systems and equipment covers the inspection, testing, preventive maintenance and survey of fire protection systems and equipment. This page lists the routine service intervals for common essential measures, by section and table number, so you can plan a year of servicing or check a contractor's program. It cites the standard and does not reproduce it, so read the activities themselves in your licensed copy.

The schedule and the edition govern

  • The fire safety schedule comes first. It lists each measure and its standard of performance. Where a measure is subject to a performance solution, maintain it to the standard that solution nominates.
  • Not everything is in AS 1851. Some essential measures, such as emergency and exit lighting, are not covered by AS 1851-2012. Maintain those to the standard in the fire safety schedule, or to their original design.
  • Edition. In NSW the applicable edition is AS 1851-2012, including amendments (currently Amendment 1, 2016). A newer edition with a different year is not adopted automatically. Standards Australia's catalogue lists AS 1851-2012 as pending revision, so check what applies before relying on these intervals.
  • Outside NSW, check your state or territory's maintenance rules and the edition they reference.

Why this matters in NSW now

From 13 February 2026, owners of new and existing class 1b and class 2 to 9 buildings in NSW must have their essential fire safety measures inspected and tested in accordance with AS 1851-2012, where the standard covers the activity. Councils enforce it. For existing buildings, Building Commission NSW says service intervals can start from 13 February 2026. It does not expect long-interval services, such as the 5-, 10- and 25-year activities, to all be done at once. It expects the first yearly service by February 2027.

Routine service intervals by measure

Intervals are taken from the service schedule headings in AS 1851-2012. Single items that run on a different interval come from the frequency matrix in the Building Commission NSW Good practice guide for implementing AS 1851-2012 (January 2026). Each schedule table lists the specific activities. Some items within a table run on a longer interval than the table's heading.

Tolerances and out-of-tolerance work

Clause 1.11 sets the frequencies (Table 1.11(A)) and a tolerance either side of each scheduled date (Table 1.11(B)). The tolerance runs from 5 working days for monthly activities and 10 working days for three-monthly activities, to 1 month for six-monthly and 2 months for yearly activities, and up to 6 months for the ten-, twenty-five- and thirty-yearly activities. The NSW guide explains that tolerances stop a year of "monthly" visits shrinking to eleven. An activity done outside its tolerance is recorded in the logbook as an out-of-tolerance activity and treated as a non-conformance. It should then be done as soon as practical.

Records you need to keep

  • Logbooks for each system (clause 1.16.3). The NSW guide says entries should reference the AS 1851 section and the interval, and record defects and corrective action. A hard copy must be left on site after each routine service, even if the contractor also records electronically.
  • Tags and labels (clause 1.16.4) for hydrants, lay flat hoses, hose reels, extinguishers, fire blankets and passive fire and smoke assets. Where tags and labels are used, a summary record (clause 1.16.5) goes to the owner.
  • Retention (clause 1.16.6). NSW owners must keep records for at least 7 years and make them available for inspection.
  • Defect reports and the yearly condition report (clause 1.17 and Appendix E). See Critical vs non-critical defects for the reporting timeframes.
  • Baseline data (clause 1.8) is limited to what the approved design required and what is needed to verify a result. The NSW guide says this means only flow and pressure data for sprinkler, hydrant and pumpset systems and some mechanical features. Where it is missing, the item is generally recorded as a non-critical defect or a non-conformance rather than a critical defect.

AS 1851 servicing is separate from the NSW annual fire safety statement, but good records make the annual assessment easier. See How to prepare an AFSS in NSW.
